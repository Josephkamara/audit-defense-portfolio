Takeaway

One audit calendar, one evidence request list, tested once by each assessor. One DR test satisfies BCP-02 and informs the SOC A1.3 availability testing. One monthly GovRAMP ConMon package reuses PCI ASV scans and internal vulnerability reports. This page shows how those activities interlock without duplicating work, and how a dashboard keeps leadership and auditors aligned on what is ready and what is not.

1. DR and BCP Audit Workpaper

Internal audit test of BCP-01, BCP-02, OPS-01 and OPS-02 for the September 2026 annual disaster recovery test. Objective: confirm that RTO and RPO targets are met, quarterly restore tests are performed with integrity checks, and backups exceed the minimum policy retention of 14 days.

The following table shows the defined targets from BCP-01.

System RTO Target RPO Target Backup Retention
Payment platform (AWS) 2 hours 5 minutes 35 days (exceeds policy minimum of 14 days)
Legacy batch system (colocation) 24 hours 24 hours (nightly backup) 35 days (exceeds policy minimum of 14 days)

The 5-minute RPO applies to the payment platform only. The legacy batch system runs settlement once a day from a nightly backup with a 24-hour RPO.

Test Steps

The following table documents the procedure steps, expected evidence and controls tested.

Step Procedure Evidence Expected
1 Inspect the approved BC/DR plan and confirm RTO and RPO targets are documented and the plan was reviewed and approved in December 2025. BC/DR plan with version history, approval signature, and December review sign-off (BCP-01).

Exception

One exception was identified during the DR test and is documented below.

Condition: The reporting schema replicated on a separate asynchronous snapshot schedule, so measured RPO was 11 minutes against the 5-minute target.

Criteria: BCP-01 defines a 5-minute RPO for the payment platform. The reporting schema is part of the payment platform system boundary.

Cause: The reporting schema was added in a 2026 release and was not added to the continuous replication configuration at that time. The schema was replicating through the nightly snapshot job instead of continuous cross-region replication.

Effect: Up to 11 minutes of reporting data could be lost in a regional failure. The transaction ledger itself met the 5-minute RPO, and transactions could still be rebuilt from GatewayCo settlement files using transaction-based recovery (NIST CP-10(2)).

Remediation: The reporting schema was moved into continuous cross-region replication through a Terraform configuration change. The change was approved in ServiceNow under CHG-01. A replication-lag CloudWatch alarm was added to alert when lag exceeds the 5-minute target.

Retest: A targeted retest on September 25, 2026 measured RPO of 2 minutes for the reporting schema. The exception was closed. Because the exception was remediated and retested within the same period, it is not recorded as a new readiness gap and the gap count remains 15.

3. GovRAMP Roadmap to Moderate

Keystone already holds GovRAMP Moderate Authorized status. This section presents the roadmap Keystone followed to Moderate and the reusable playbook for bringing an acquired business into scope (see section 6).

GovRAMP is a nonprofit that verifies cloud security for state and local governments. Its baselines are built on NIST SP 800-53 Rev 5. GovRAMP was formerly StateRAMP. The rebrand was announced in February 2025 and the legal name remains StateRAMP, operating as GovRAMP.

GovRAMP terminology and statuses:

  • Security Snapshot: An initial maturity scoring by the GovRAMP PMO.
  • Progressing Security Snapshot Program: A provider that is not yet verified can be listed as progressing and is re-scored on a regular cadence while it closes gaps.
  • Verified statuses: GovRAMP Core (a smaller focused control set assessed by the PMO, no 3PAO), Ready (3PAO readiness assessment), Provisionally Authorized, and Authorized (full 3PAO security assessment, PMO validation). Verified products appear on GovRAMP's published product list.
  • Continuous monitoring: Authorized providers submit continuous monitoring monthly (POA&M, scan results, inventory) and complete an annual assessment by a 3PAO.
  • 3PAO: A third-party assessment organization registered with GovRAMP and accredited by A2LA. GovRAMP adopted the same 3PAO accreditation standard FedRAMP uses.

GRC Platform Integration

GovRAMP is set up in the GRC platform as a custom framework. Because GovRAMP baselines are built on NIST SP 800-53 Rev 5 and closely track FedRAMP, the team cloned the platform's FedRAMP Moderate (800-53 Rev 5) control mappings as the starting point, then adjusted for GovRAMP-specific requirements. This means one control and one evidence item still map to PCI DSS, SOC 2 and GovRAMP, and the GRC platform pulls automated evidence for all three programs from the same source (AWS Config, Okta, GitHub, ServiceNow).

6. Integrating an Acquired Company

A 30/60/90 compliance onboarding plan for bringing an acquired business into the Keystone compliance program. The acquired business (not named) must be scoped, controls mapped to the unified matrix, gaps tracked, auditors notified, and systems added to the recurring evidence collection cycle.

Days Focus Outputs
Days 1 to 30 Inventory systems, data flows and card data exposure; decide whether the acquired systems enter the PCI CDE, the SOC system description and the GovRAMP boundary, or stay separated; collect existing reports (AOC, SOC reports, pen tests); put Okta SSO and MFA and central logging in place first; freeze new connections to the Keystone CDE until segmentation is tested. System inventory, data flow diagrams, boundary decision memo, collected reports, Okta SSO and MFA rollout plan, logging integration plan, connection freeze policy
Days 31 to 60 Map the acquired business's controls to the unified control matrix; add gaps to the remediation tracker with owners and dates; notify the QSA, SOC auditor and GovRAMP PMO of significant change where required (scope confirmation 12.5.2.1 on significant change; GovRAMP significant change process); update contract obligations if agency customers move over. Control mapping workpaper, new gaps in the tracker, scope confirmation memos to QSA and SOC auditor, GovRAMP significant change notification, updated contract obligations register
Days 61 to 90 Run a readiness check, a segmentation test if connected, add the systems to the next ConMon inventory, the next access review and the PBC list; brief leadership using the section 5 dashboard. Readiness assessment, segmentation test report (if connected), updated inventory workbook, access review scope expansion, PBC list updates, leadership dashboard showing the expanded scope

The controls count on this page does not change until the acquired systems are formally brought into scope. If the acquired business has separate compliance obligations, the unified calendar and shared evidence list remain the same until integration is complete.

The GovRAMP roadmap in section 3 serves as the reusable playbook: Security Snapshot, gap remediation with a POA&M, readiness assessment by a 3PAO if needed, and then continuous monitoring. The same phases apply when integrating an acquired business into an existing GovRAMP authorization.
