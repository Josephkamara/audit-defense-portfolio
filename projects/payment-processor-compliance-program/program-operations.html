Exception

One exception was identified during the DR test and is documented below.

Condition: The reporting schema replicated on a separate asynchronous snapshot schedule, so measured RPO was 11 minutes against the 5-minute target.

Criteria: BCP-01 defines a 5-minute RPO for the payment platform. The reporting schema is part of the payment platform system boundary.

Cause: The reporting schema was added in a 2026 release and was not added to the continuous replication configuration at that time. The schema was copied to the DR region by a separate 10-minute asynchronous snapshot job instead of continuous cross-region replication.

Effect: Up to 11 minutes of reporting data could be lost in a regional failure. The transaction ledger itself met the 5-minute RPO, and transactions could still be rebuilt from GatewayCo settlement files using transaction-based recovery (NIST CP-10(2)).

Remediation: The reporting schema was moved into continuous cross-region replication through a Terraform configuration change. The Terraform pull request, with plan output attached, was peer reviewed under branch protection (CHG-02), linked to a ServiceNow change record approved by the change advisory board (CHG-01), and applied by the GitHub Actions deployment role (CHG-05). A replication-lag CloudWatch alarm was added to alert when lag exceeds the 5-minute target.

Retest: A targeted retest on September 25, 2026 measured RPO of 2 minutes for the reporting schema. The exception was closed. Because the exception was remediated and retested within the same period, it is not recorded as a new readiness gap and the gap count remains 15.

3. GovRAMP Roadmap to Moderate

Keystone already holds GovRAMP Moderate Authorized status. This section presents the roadmap Keystone followed to Moderate and the reusable playbook for bringing an acquired business into scope (see section 6).

GovRAMP is a nonprofit that verifies cloud security for state and local governments. Its baselines are built on NIST SP 800-53 Rev 5. GovRAMP was formerly StateRAMP. The rebrand was announced in February 2025 and the legal name remains StateRAMP, operating as GovRAMP.

GovRAMP terminology and statuses:

  • Security Snapshot: An initial maturity scoring by the GovRAMP PMO.
  • Progressing Security Snapshot Program: A provider that is not yet verified can join this program, is re-scored quarterly, and appears on GovRAMP's Progressing Product List while it closes gaps.
  • Verified statuses: GovRAMP Core (a smaller focused control set assessed by the PMO, no 3PAO), Ready (3PAO readiness assessment), Provisionally Authorized, and Authorized (full 3PAO security assessment, PMO validation). Verified products appear on GovRAMP's published product list.
  • Continuous monitoring: Authorized providers submit continuous monitoring monthly (POA&M, scan results, inventory) and complete an annual assessment by a 3PAO.
  • 3PAO: A third-party assessment organization registered with GovRAMP and accredited by A2LA. GovRAMP adopted the same 3PAO accreditation standard FedRAMP uses.

7. Management Testing and Auditor Reliance

Keystone's external auditors test the same key controls every year: the service auditor for SOC 1 and SOC 2, the QSA for PCI DSS, and the 3PAO for GovRAMP. Management testing means the GRC team tests those controls on a set schedule, using the same test procedures, sample sizes and evidence the auditors use, and keeps workpapers an auditor can re-perform. The goals are to find and fix exceptions before the auditors do, to hand auditors clean evidence, and, where the standards allow it, to have the auditors use Keystone's work so they test less and charge less. Management testing is performed by the IT auditor in the GRC team and reviewed by the Director of GRC. The GRC team reports to the CIO, and Keystone has no separate internal audit function. The reliance plan below is written with that limit in mind.

7.3 Reliance Plan by Audit

Each audit has different rules about whether an external auditor can use Keystone's own testing. The plan below asks each auditor for what their standards allow and nothing more.

Audit What the standards allow What Keystone asks for Expected effect
SOC 1 and SOC 2 (service auditor) Under the AICPA attestation standards (AT-C section 205 for SOC 2 and AT-C section 320 for SOC 1, with AT-C section 105), the service auditor may use the work of an internal audit function, or use internal audit or management testers to provide direct assistance under the auditor's direction, supervision and review. First the auditor evaluates the testers' competence and objectivity (and, to use a function's work, whether it follows a systematic, disciplined approach). The auditor stays solely responsible for the opinion and re-performs some of the work. Direct assistance or use of management testing for routine, lower-risk controls: ACC-04, ACC-01, ACC-02, CHG-01, OPS-01, OPS-02, OPS-03. Fewer auditor samples and fieldwork hours on those controls, which supports a lower fee.
PCI DSS (QSA) The QSA must verify every applicable requirement through its own testing and observation. PCI DSS has no formal process for the QSA to rely on Keystone's testing in place of its own. The QSA does test GOV-06 (PCI DSS 12.4.2 and 12.4.2.1, quarterly reviews that personnel follow security procedures), and management's quarterly test results are evidence for that requirement. An agreed evidence request list and sample approach at the October ROC planning meeting; management workpapers offered as supporting evidence. Shorter fieldwork and fewer follow-up requests. Not formal reliance, and no reduction in the QSA's required testing.
GovRAMP (3PAO) The 3PAO must be independent of Keystone and tests the controls in its own security assessment plan. The annual assessment covers a subset of controls plus required items. Keystone's testing cannot replace 3PAO testing. Use of the monthly continuous monitoring deliverables (scans, POA&M, inventory) and management test workpapers as supporting evidence when the 3PAO scopes and samples. Fewer surprises, faster evidence review and fewer follow-up requests during the December assessment.

Objectivity note. Management testing at Keystone is done by the IT auditor in the GRC team, which reports to the CIO, and there is no separate internal audit team reporting to the audit committee. An auditor will see the testers as less objective than an independent internal audit function. The realistic outcome is that the service auditor uses Keystone's work on routine, lower-risk controls such as access reviews, backups and change tickets, and still tests high-risk or judgment-heavy controls itself, such as the risk assessment (GOV-03), PAN protection (CRY-01), log review (LOG-03) and penetration testing (VUL-04). GRC-operated controls (GOV-06, GOV-07, GOV-08) are always tested directly by the auditors. To improve objectivity over time, the IT auditor does not operate any control being tested, workpapers are reviewed by the Director of GRC, and a summary of management test results goes to the Risk Committee each quarter.
